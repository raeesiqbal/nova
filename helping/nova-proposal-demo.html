Your lease obligations

Full Repairing Leases: understanding your legal position
The liability your lease may already place on you — and how to turn it to your advantage
If your occupation of this site is governed by a full repairing lease, the obligation to maintain the building envelope — including the roof — sits with you as the tenant. This is not a discretionary spend. A full repairing lease requires you to return the property in no worse condition than at the start of your term, and an ageing or failing roof will be assessed and costed against you at the point of dilapidations. The question is not whether you pay for the roof. It is whether you pay on your terms or on the landlord's.
The cost of delay
A dilapidations claim on a failing commercial roof is typically assessed at full replacement cost plus surveyors' fees. The landlord's surveyor will not apply a patch. You pay for a new roof regardless — but at a time and price not of your choosing.
The opportunity within the obligation
Addressing the roof now, on your terms, means you control the specification and the outcome. Combining it with solar transforms a liability into a revenue-generating asset for the remainder of your occupation.
A note on landlord engagement. A new roof extends the asset life of their property. Solar adds a modern, low-carbon credential supporting ESG reporting and future letting value. Nova can assist in framing the commercial case to a landlord where this is relevant to your situation.

The cost of inaction

A deteriorating roof is not a passive risk
Your statutory obligations under health and safety law do not pause while a decision is deferred
Commercial and industrial roof systems degrade in ways that are not always visible from inside the building. By the time water ingress, panel deflection, or structural fatigue becomes apparent at ground level, the risk has already been present for some time. As the occupier of this site, you carry a duty of care under the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974 and the Workplace (Health, Safety and Welfare) Regulations 1992. A roof in poor condition is a foreseeable hazard — and foreseeability is the test that matters in both HSE enforcement and civil litigation.
Water ingress
Creates slip hazards, electrical risks, and stock or equipment damage. Each incident is a documented liability event that sits on your record.
Fragile roof panels
Many older commercial roof sheets become fragile with age. Any maintenance access creates a risk of fall-through carrying serious criminal exposure under the Work at Height Regulations 2005.
Structural fatigue
Deformation of purlins or sheeting rails is rarely visible without inspection. Continued loading on a fatigued structure risks progressive and sudden failure.
